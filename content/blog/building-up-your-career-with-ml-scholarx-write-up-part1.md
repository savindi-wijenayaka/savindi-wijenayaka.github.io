### The first step in ML

#### 1. Maths

In many courses, I have seen people say, you don’t want Maths. Yes, for mere manipulation of a pre-trained model, we don’t want Maths. However, to do proper professional research on Model Architecture, Algorithms, etc., you must have an understanding of the internal mechanisms. That requires fair knowledge about Maths, particularly in the following areas

- Probabilities
- Matrices
- Algebra
- Calculus

#### 2. Knowing the fundamentals

As Dr. Rukshan always tell;

> Knowing the fundamentals is the key to success

For example, if you don’t know what softmax is, you can’t go ahead and blindly put it when you are creating a model in Keras. Or, if you don’t know what tokenization is, you can’t go ahead and do a Question and Answering system. So the key is to have a proper understanding of the fundamentals of ML.

I have written a post on Introduction to ML, named [Basics of Machine Learning](https://medium.com/@phantomgrin/ml-101-part-1-basics-of-machine-learning-1734836696c1), before. To have the general idea of ML you can go ahead and read it. Also, I strongly suggest [Google ML crash course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/ml-intro). It is a bit of a reading-oriented one, even though it has videos included. Another great course I personally suggest is [mlcourse.ai](https://mlcourse.ai/). This one actually should follow after the Google course, since it is focusing on going ***deep in Theories*** with ***practicals*** more than covering the whole flow of ML.

Another two very good courses I will always suggest is [Andrew Ng’s course on Coursera regarding ML](https://www.coursera.org/learn/machine-learning) and Stanford University Courses (there are many separate topics like CNN, NLP, reinforcement learning, etc. You can find it in youtube). Go for Stanford after you are sure that you covered enough basics.

You can find many more resources and good courses in [Robbie Allen](https://medium.com/u/c308c421ca8d)’s post; [My Curated List of AI and Machine Learning Resources from Around the Web](https://medium.com/machine-learning-in-practice/my-curated-list-of-ai-and-machine-learning-resources-from-around-the-web-9a97823b8524).

### Higher Education in ML

Many university undergraduates who are enthusiastic about ML, usually have the dream of pursuing higher studies in a sub-domain of Machine learning. Usually, we Sri Lankans plan to do a PhD in a foreign country since the field of ML is still in its infant stage in Sri Lanka. So how can we get into a Foreign University, with a full or partial scholarship?

#### 1. Graduate with a First Class

Keeping up your Grade Point Average (GPA) is one key aspect in scoring points on scholarships. If you manage to get a First Class (or at least Second Upper), you have successfully taken one step towards your dream University.

#### 2. Choose a Good Topic for your final year Research

With the time, trend of researches changes. However, you can do two kinds of researches.

1. Theoretical Research → Researches on aspects like architectures, best loss functions, best optimization algorithms, etc. falls under this. Simply you are trying to redefine or modify theories in ML. Requires a thorough understanding of fundamentals.
2. Applied Research → Researches which tries to solve a practical issue in an application domain like Medicine, Accountancy, Education, etc.

Both types are good. In the current trend, the best pick for a Research topic will be [Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_adversarial_network) or [Deep Reinforcement Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_reinforcement_learning). Also, areas, where fewer researches are done (Ex: [NLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing)), is also quite lucrative.

If you are interested in Applied research, try an application domain that has a lesser amount of research, like Medicine, Education, etc. This will give you a higher chance to publish your research and present in a Conference.

> The most important aspect that you need to consider when selecting a Research Topic is, **TIME**

You need to think about how much you know and how much you need to know to complete the research successfully. Basically your knowledge gap. Then consider the speed of your learning ability. compare it with the knowledge gap to see whether you have enough time.

#### 3. Publish your research

Try to complete your final year research in an outstanding manner. Then apply for conferences, Global and Local. It will be extremely hard to present in a Global conference but always try. Some grate conferences include;

1. [ICML](https://icml.cc/)
2. [NEURIPS](https://nips.cc/)
3. [ICCV](http://iccv2019.thecvf.com/)
4. [KDD](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2019/)
5. [IEEE](https://www.ieee.org/)

You can find more about conferences from [George Seif](https://medium.com/u/e2af5c8737ec)’s “[The Top Upcoming AI Conferences in 2019](https://towardsdatascience.com/the-top-upcoming-ai-conferences-in-2019-3c3eb6f4c515)”.

At least try to get your research published in the local IEEE conference for Machine Learning.
